When I joined Evotix as a Lead Developer, my first responsibility was taking ownership of the WordPress backend for a headless learning and training platform. The backend already served content and functionality through APIs to React frontends. Since then, my role has grown from leading development of that system to helping re-architect the wider platform around a React frontend and cloud-native backend infrastructure.

The re-architecture has two primary goals: replatforming to cloud-native infrastructure, and creating a foundation that can support future growth. That means more than moving the existing system somewhere new. We have taken the opportunity to revisit and significantly redefine features, making deliberate choices about how the platform should evolve and ensuring the foundations are forward-compatible.

Improving maintainability and reducing legacy complexity are important outcomes too, but the migration itself is one of the biggest risks. Existing systems support real users and business needs; they contain years of decisions, integrations and knowledge. Replacing them means protecting that value while moving toward a new platform. Before changing the shape of a system, it matters to understand what it does well, where the friction is, and which constraints are real.

That changes the question from “How do we rewrite this?” to “How do we create the right foundation, and move to it safely?” Clearer boundaries between the frontend and backend can help teams reason about each part independently. The migration needs to be planned and validated carefully, with attention to how existing capabilities and user needs carry forward. A deliberate approach gives the product room to continue moving while the underlying architecture changes.

The technical work is only one part of it. A sustainable architecture needs agreement across the people building and supporting it. That means explaining trade-offs, connecting technical improvements to product goals, and giving the team a shared understanding of what is changing and why.

For me, this work has reinforced that good architecture is not a diagram or a technology list. It is a system that people can understand, change and operate with confidence. Replatforming, redesigning features for future growth and managing migration risk all serve the same goal: a stronger foundation for what comes next.
